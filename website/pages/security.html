Privacy and security

What Phone Mirror does, what it never does, and how to check for yourself.

In short

InternetOnly for one anonymous count a day. Everything else the app does stays between your phone and your Mac, over the cable or your own Wi-Fi. No update checks, no crash reports.
AccountNone. No sign-up, no login, no email address.
Usage countAnonymous, and you can switch it off. Once a day the app tells TelemetryDeck (a privacy-focused analytics service for apps) that a copy of Phone Mirror was opened, and whether it mirrored a phone or received a Quick Share that day. A new copy also says once that it is new. With it go only the app version, the macOS version and the chip type, under a random number made on your Mac and scrambled (hashed) before it is sent. No name, no email, nothing about your phone, your Wi-Fi, your screen or your files. Like every request on the internet it comes from your internet address; the app adds nothing that identifies you. To stop it, untick Send anonymous usage count in the menu: nothing more is sent and the random number is deleted.

Why macOS warns you the first time

When you open an app from the internet, macOS checks whether Apple has examined it. Apple offers that check to developers who pay for its yearly developer programme. I make Phone Mirror on my own, and it has not been through that programme.

So macOS says it “could not verify” the app. That sentence means Apple has not looked at it. It does not mean something was found. macOS then leaves the decision to you, in System Settings → Privacy & Security. The download page shows the steps.

You shouldn't have to take my word for any of this, so the next part is what you can check yourself.

What is inside the app

Phone Mirror is a small Mac app that starts two well-known programs for you and gives them a friendly menu:

  • scrcpy, by Genymobile. It shows and controls the phone's screen. It is open source: anyone can read its code.
  • adb, Android's own tool for connecting a phone to a computer, from the Android Open Source Project. The copy inside the app is the one built and signed by Google.

Each time it starts, Phone Mirror asks macOS to check that nothing inside the app has been swapped or edited since it was built. If that check fails, it refuses to run those two programs.

About USB debugging

USB debugging is the Android setting that makes all of this possible. It is powerful, so it is worth knowing what it does.

  • It lets a computer that you have approved see the screen, send clicks and keys, and copy files. A computer you have not approved gets nothing.
  • The phone asks you before it trusts a computer. Only tap Allow for your own Mac. Never for a computer you don't know, and never at a public charging point.
  • To take the trust back: Developer options → Revoke USB debugging authorisations.
  • When you stop using Phone Mirror for good, switch USB debugging off again.

About wireless mode

  • While wireless mode is on, the phone listens for your Mac on the network it is connected to. Use it on your home Wi-Fi or your own hotspot, not on public Wi-Fi.
  • Phone Mirror only connects to addresses on a private home or office network. It refuses anything else.
  • The menu shows a warning for as long as wireless mode is on. Disconnect Wireless switches it off, and by default the app also switches it off when you quit.
  • Restarting the phone switches wireless mode off by itself.

About receiving with Quick Share

  • It is off until you tick it in the menu. While it is off, Phone Mirror does not listen on the network at all.
  • While it is on, your Mac's name is visible to devices on the same Wi-Fi, and they can ask to send you something. This is like AirDrop set to Everyone. Untick it on Wi-Fi you don't trust.
  • Only devices on the same local network can connect. Connections from the internet are refused.
  • Nothing is saved until you click Accept, for every single share. The card shows who is sending, what, and a four-digit code. If your phone shows a code too, the two should match.
  • The transfer is encrypted between the phone and the Mac.
  • Files go to your Downloads folder. A file that is already there is never replaced, and a transfer that breaks off leaves no half file behind.
  • Received files are marked as downloads, so macOS checks an app or a script before it runs for the first time, the same way it does for a download from a browser.

What macOS may ask you

Phone Mirror does not need screen recording, accessibility, camera or microphone access, and never asks for them. You may see these questions, each only when you use that feature:

  • Find devices on your local network: when you go wireless or switch on Quick Share. That is how the Mac and the phone reach each other.
  • Accept incoming network connections: only if your Mac's firewall is on and you switch on Quick Share.
  • Access your Desktop, Documents or Downloads folder: when you copy a file to or from one of them.
  • Open at login: if you turn on “Launch at login” in the menu.
